Settling a vase order

A vase is bought for the arrangement it will hold rather than the shape on its own, so the mouth, the foot and the packing are what get fixed first.

Mouth width and the arrangement it takes

A narrow mouth holds one stem or a tulip upright; a medium mouth takes a hand-tied bouquet; a wide shallow bowl takes branches. The mouth also decides whether a sheet of foam can be added as an option when the arrangement needs extra support, but it is not standard on every piece.

Foot weight and where it stands

Most bodies stand on their own once the foot is proportioned to the height; only very tall or narrow-necked pieces need a separate base or mat.

Cartons, sleeves and breakage

Branch vases ship in a sleeve with a guard over the opening; a pallet of sleeved bodies breaks far less than a pallet of loose ones. Event orders often mix one tall and one low height on the same tray.

How crystal vases are made

The same four stages carry every order, from one vase to a full container of mixed heights. Seeing them once is usually enough to plan the delivery.

Material selection

The run starts with the block. Clarity, inclusions and the optical tone are chosen before cutting, because the material decides how the facets will read once polished.

Opening the mouth and shoulder

The block is cut to a blank and roughed on the mouth and shoulder profile, then the wall is opened out and the profile brought down to the foot.

Bringing the faces up

The wall is taken through the grades, coarse to final, which is what keeps a clear bowl instead of a milky one. The rim is rolled here so the lip will not flare when filled.

Wrapping and boxing

Laser, sandblast or etch work is placed over the approved artwork, the piece is checked against the sample, then wrapped at the mouth and boxed for export.

What you can customise on vases

The list below is what buyers most often specify. Anything on it is quoted per unit, so the clearer your brief at the start, the faster the quotation.

  • Opening — mouth diameter and depth matched to the stem type you normally use
  • Weight and wall thickness — heavier walls read as more expensive in photos and in the hand
  • Silhouette — tapered, cylindrical, faceted, ribbed, bowed or fully bespoke
  • Facet and polish level — full mirror polish, matte frosted or a mixed satin-plus-mirror finish
  • Colour — clear, smoked, amber, blue or a gradient through the body
  • Base and stability — a flat felt pad, a metal collar, a wooden disc or a weighted foot
  • Packaging — single-piece drop test box, master carton or retail-ready sleeve

Frequently asked questions about crystal vases

Are crystal vases practical for everyday.use?
They are, as long as the wall thickness and base are specified for daily use. We will recommend a weight and foot that keeps the vase stable on a table but still looks refined on camera.
Can the vase be produced with a printed or engraved logo?
Yes. A discreet logo on the base or a lower panel works well for hotel groups and corporate gift lines; full-surface branding is possible for promotional programmes.
Do you supply the vases ready for retail shelf?
We supply retail-ready cartons with the piece individually protected, plus a bar-code label and a printed insert if your system requires them.
Will water leave marks or a ring on the surface?
A polished crystal surface shows water spots more than a matte one. We can specify a matte lower body or an easy-care polish so the piece survives daily refill in a public space.
Can you match a vase we already sell?
Send the reference piece or a drawing with the critical dimensions — height, mouth, base diameter and weight — and we will quote a close or an exact alternative.
How do you pack something this fragile?
Each vase ships in a moulded or foam-lined box with the neck and base separately braced, inside a double-wall master carton sized to the order.

Still deciding? Send us the design, the quantity and the date you need it by, and we will come back with a quotation and a production plan.
